MaterialIQ: Objective Quality in the Shopping Cart

An AI-powered browser extension that analyzes product quality, material composition, and value for money while shopping online - removing uncertainty by providing objective, transparent scoring for products across the web.

The browser interface: MaterialIQ parsing active e-commerce details to show material and price scores directly in the shopping cart.
01. Problem

The Challenge: Product Integrity in E-Commerce

Consumers spend hundreds of dollars on products assuming high quality, often struggling to understand complex material blends, construction details, and fair pricing models. E-commerce platforms intentionally obfuscate material breakdowns, relying on brand perception and high prices to imply durability.

Without objective data, price is falsely equated with quality. MaterialIQ aims to remove that uncertainty by providing objective, transparent scoring for products directly on the product page.

02. Constraints

Parsing Unstructured Data in Real-Time

The primary constraint was operating as a low-friction browser extension. It needed to parse highly unstructured, wildly varying e-commerce product descriptions and specifications in real-time, without forcing the user to wait for slow cloud inference.

1. Unstructured Web DOM

Every e-commerce site formats its material specifications differently. The system required robust DOM extraction to locate hidden fabric breakdowns without breaking when sites updated their layouts.

2. Low Latency Inference

Shoppers move quickly. The extension had to deliver material analysis and scoring near-instantly directly inside the shopping cart.

3. Transparent Scoring

Black-box "quality scores" breed distrust. The methodology had to be mathematically clear, exposing exactly why an item received its score.

03. Approach

Algorithmic Material & Value Analysis

I engineered an AI-powered Chrome extension backed by a high-performance FastAPI backend. The system evaluates products based on three objective pillars:

01

Material Quality Score

Analysis based on fiber composition, fabric weight, and raw material grading. The system mathematically penalizes cheap synthetic blends marketed at luxury price points.

02

Durability Estimate

Inferred lifespan based on the extracted materials and construction cues (e.g., natural vs. synthetic ratios, weave density).

03

Value Score

An objective calculation determining if the item is actually worth its asking price based on the raw material cost baseline.

04. Outcome

Beta Launch & Transparent Methodology

Launched MaterialIQ (Beta) on the Chrome Web Store. The extension successfully deployed with Skimlinks integration and a strict privacy policy, parsing thousands of products and bringing objective transparency to the shopping experience.

System Architecture & Tech Stack

05. Lessons Learned

Building Consumer Trust Through Clarity

1. Transparency is the Feature: Users do not trust a black-box "Quality Score" - they need to see the exact fiber breakdowns and the rule-based logic that drove the calculation. Making the methodology transparent was critical to adoption.

2. Handling Unstructured Chaos: Parsing unstructured e-commerce DOMs taught me the importance of robust fallback mechanisms. When a site layout breaks an extraction rule, the system must degrade gracefully and clearly communicate what data is missing, rather than generating an incorrect score.
