01. Problem

The Challenge: Non-Deterministic LLM Failure Modes

Large Language Models (LLMs) deployed in production exhibit non-deterministic failure modes that automated benchmark suites (such as MMLU or GSM8K) routinely fail to catch. These failures include subtle instruction drift across long dialog turns, hallucination of domain-specific specifications, and logical fallacies under complex multi-constraint prompts.

Without continuous, rigorous empirical evaluation, enterprise deployments risk deploying models that fail unpredictably when users venture into edge-case scenarios or high-stakes workflows.

02. Constraints

Multi-Turn Windows & Evolving Checkpoints

1. Multi-Turn Context Windows

Evaluations must assess model performance across extended conversations where context degradation and goal drift accumulate gradually over multiple interaction turns.

2. High Throughput & Precision

Models must be audited rapidly across hundreds of prompts while maintaining granular scoring consistency across factual accuracy, logical reasoning, and safety alignment.

3. Evolving Model Checkpoints

Evaluation frameworks must provide consistent metrics across rapid model iterations (RLHF/SFT passes) to verify that fixing one failure mode does not introduce regressions elsewhere.

03. Approach

Granular Taxonomy & Closed-Loop Telemetry

Contracted with leading AI research labs (Handshake AI) to execute systematic empirical evaluations of generative LLM checkpoints.

01

Error Taxonomy Classification

Engineered a structured error classification taxonomy isolating hallucination types, logical inconsistency, constraint violations, and tone/style misalignments.

02

Adversarial Edge-Case Stress Testing

Designed multi-step prompts intentionally constructed to expose context boundaries, instruction conflicts, and edge-case failure modes.

03

Closed-Loop Telemetry to Research Teams

Transformed qualitative evaluation observations into quantitative telemetry reports, returning structured feedback directly to model training teams to accelerate alignment cycles.

05. Lessons Learned

Reflection & AI Safety Takeaways

1. Automated Benchmarks Are Insufficient for Reliability: Standard automated evaluations (like MMLU) fail to detect subtle human-perceived reasoning failures; continuous expert human auditing is non-negotiable for safety-critical AI.

2. Taxonomy Quality Determines Training Quality: Clear, unambiguous error taxonomies are essential—without precise error categorization, model trainers cannot pinpoint the root causes of non-deterministic model failures.
