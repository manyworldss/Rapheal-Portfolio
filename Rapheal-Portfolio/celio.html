Celio

An offline iPhone app that helps people with celiac disease explain their needs to the person cooking their food, in their language, and hear back. No account. Less friction at the table.

CHAPTER 01

Problem

Explaining celiac disease across a language barrier is more than translating a sentence. The person preparing the food needs to understand the restriction and cross-contact, while the person asking needs a clear way to hear back. That exchange happens at restaurants, dinner parties, and on delivery orders.

CHAPTER 02

Constraints

  • Work offline, without an account or a reliable connection.
  • Keep the dietary message readable when a phone is handed to someone else.
  • Make room for a reply, rather than treating communication as a one-way card.
  • Keep the public website focused on explaining the iOS product.
CHAPTER 03

Approach

Ran 10+ interviews and surveyed 100+ people with celiac disease about dining barriers while traveling. Built a native iOS app in Swift and SwiftUI from those findings, then iterated on beta feedback. The current direction centers on presenting a clear dietary card, choosing the other person’s language, and supporting a two-way exchange. The marketing site explains that focused experience before someone installs the app.

CHAPTER 04

Outcome

The native iOS app is in TestFlight with 50+ beta users across 3+ countries, with an App Store release coming soon. The public repository now contains a lightweight static marketing site. The former Django version is preserved under the django-web-app-final tag.

CHAPTER 05

Lessons Learned

A focused communication aid is easier to explain and use than a collection of travel features. The central design question is whether two people can understand one another in the moment they need to.
